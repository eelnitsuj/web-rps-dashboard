Where in the funnel it broke — checkout completion, on July 7

Checkout completion held at ~52% for three straight months, then fell off a cliff on Jul 7. Apr 52.3%, May 52.5%, Jun 8 – Jul 5 52.0% — then Jul 7 37.4%, Jul 8 33.9%, Jul 9 32.8%. Cart-add and reach-checkout rates went up in those same days, so shoppers were not losing interest — they were getting to checkout and failing to finish. That is a checkout, payment, or shipping-step failure, not a traffic-quality or demand problem. It has since partly healed to ~45%, still about 7 points under baseline.

Two separate events, not one

This is the answer to "the bots were cleared, so why is RPS still at a low?" There were two independent things happening, and comparing across both of them at once produces a wrong answer. Split into three windows, the Escape Hatch removal did exactly what it was supposed to: against the window immediately before it, sessions fell 35% while revenue rose 3%, so RPS jumped from $0.71 to $1.13. Those sessions were genuinely phantom. What the removal did not fix is a separate revenue decline that began around Aug 9 — revenue per day fell from ~$9,465 to ~$7,000 and stayed there — which is why RPS is still far below the pre-break baseline.

Daily RPS — where the level shift actually lands

The all-time-low weekly bar for the week of Aug 10 is an aggregation artifact: it blends the contaminated Aug 9–12 stretch with the clean Aug 13–15 days. At daily grain the actual low is Aug 9 ($0.46, the Google bot spike), and the post-removal days have recovered to roughly $1.13 — still well below the $1.30 that Aug 1–8 was running.

Verdicts

Confirmed
The Escape Hatch removal worked. It is not why RPS is low.

Measured against the window immediately before it (Aug 9–12, when sessions were inflated), the removal cut sessions 35% per day while revenue per day rose 3% and orders rose 4%. RPS went $0.71 → $1.13, a 59% recovery, and AOV moved −0.6%. That is precisely the signature of removing non-converting traffic: the denominator falls, the numerator doesn't.

An intermediate version of this report claimed the opposite — that the removal cost revenue — on a Thu–Sat comparison of Aug 6–8 against Aug 13–15. That was wrong. Aug 6 and 7 were the two strongest revenue days in August, and a separate decline had already begun on Aug 9, so the comparison anchored to a peak and attributed a pre-existing drop to the Aug 13 event. Day-of-week matching does not protect against a level shift landing mid-window.

Open — the actual problem
Web revenue stepped down around Aug 9 and has not recovered.

Revenue per day fell from $9,465 (Aug 1–8) to roughly $7,000 and has stayed there through Aug 15 — orders per day −16% and AOV −9% against Aug 1–8. This is independent of the Aug 13 removal and is what actually holds RPS 41% below the June baseline.

The AOV half of it is not a step at all but the tail of a four-week fade: weekly web AOV peaked at $65.56 (week of Jul 20) and has declined every week since — $64.52, $63.07, $59.13 — back through the $59.20 June baseline. Whatever lifted order value after Jun 7 has fully worn off, so there is no longer anything cushioning the conversion decline. What changed on or around Aug 9 is not identified in this dataset.

Corrected — the owner was right
The pre-break baseline is 2.84%, squarely inside the remembered 2.5–3%.

An earlier version of this report called that memory refuted, on the basis that Jun 8 – Jul 5 measured 3.16%. That window was a local peak, not the norm. Across the full stable period Apr 1 – Jul 6, sitewide conversion is 2.84% — April 2.63%, May 2.86%, Jun 1–6 2.65%. The remembered range was accurate and the 3.16% figure was an artifact of the window I was originally handed.

This matters for sizing: against 2.84% rather than 3.16%, the conversion decline is \u221232%, not \u221245%. Still severe, but a third smaller than previously stated.

Refuted — twice over
AOV is flat. It never explained anything.

Against the pre-break baseline of $59.15 (Apr 1 – Jul 6), current AOV is $58.60 — −0.9%. Order value has sat between $58 and $60 for four and a half months. The +26% implied by dividing −40% RPS by −52% CVR was never real; nor was the +6.5% I reported off the narrower June window; nor was the mid-July bump to $65.56, which was a four-week excursion that fully reverted.

With AOV flat, the decomposition is trivial: RPS −32.8% is almost entirely CVR −32.1%. There is no order-value story here at all.

Open — not yet answerable
Cohort LTV cannot judge the June 7 change for about two more weeks.

Through month 1 — the only maturity where all three relevant cohorts are complete — the June cohort is ahead: $59.34 per customer against $56.17 (May) and $57.65 (April). Initial order value per customer also rose across the same cohorts, $46.03 → $46.32 → $48.38.

But month 2 for the June cohort and month 1 for the July cohort both land in August, which is only ~52% elapsed. Neither is readable yet. The first clean month-2 read on a post-change cohort arrives at the start of September.

The volume side is not ambiguous. New customers acquired: April 12,527 → May 8,310 → June 9,000 → July 6,406. July is down 29% against June. Slightly better per-customer economics on materially fewer customers is not obviously a win, and whether it nets out depends on what happened to CAC — which is not in this dataset.
