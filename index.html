Why RPS did not recover after the Escape Hatch came off

This is the answer to "the bots were cleared, so why is RPS still at a low?" Removing sessions should mechanically raise revenue per session, because the denominator shrinks and the numerator shouldn't care. That is not what happened. Day-of-week-matched, Thu–Sat before (Aug 6–8) against Thu–Sat after (Aug 13–15): sessions fell 18.5%, but web revenue fell 32.4%. Revenue fell roughly twice as fast as traffic, so RPS went down 17%, not up. Sessions that convert at all are not phantom sessions.

Daily RPS around the two events — the weekly view was hiding this

The all-time-low weekly bar for the week of Aug 10 is an aggregation artifact: it blends the contaminated Aug 9–12 stretch with the clean Aug 13–15 days. At daily grain the actual low is Aug 9 ($0.46, the Google bot spike), and the post-removal days have recovered to roughly $1.13 — still well below the $1.30 that Aug 1–8 was running.

Verdicts

Revised — supersedes the earlier "phantom sessions confirmed" read
The Escape Hatch traffic was converting. Removing it cost real revenue.

The earlier version of this report confirmed ~19% phantom-session inflation on session counts alone and predicted that stripping them would lift conversion from 2.09% to 2.57%. With revenue joined in, that prediction fails. Thu–Sat matched: sessions −18.5%, orders −25.7%, revenue −32.4%. Conversion fell to 1.93% and AOV fell from $64.49 to $58.60.

Both halves of RPS got worse at once. If those sessions had been bots, removing them would have left orders and revenue untouched and lifted every ratio. Instead the removal took revenue out faster than it took traffic out. Caveat worth holding: this is three days against three days, and Aug 6 was August's single best revenue day, so the baseline is flattering. The direction is clear; the magnitude is not yet settled.

Refuted
The pre-July baseline was 3.16%, not the 2.5–3% remembered.

Measured sitewide conversion for Jun 8 – Jul 5 is 3.16%; the spot weeks check out at 3.18% (Jun 22) and 3.44% (Jun 29). No day in the June baseline fell below 2.65%. The decline is larger than the remembered baseline implies, not smaller.

Refuted
AOV is up ~6.5%, not the +26% implied by −40% ÷ −52%.

Aligned to one window and one channel (Online Store, week of Aug 3 vs. June baseline): RPS −41.6%, CVR −45.1%, AOV +6.5%, reconciling exactly at 0.549 × 1.065 = 0.585. The +26% came from dividing an RPS and a CVR measured over different windows. On Shopify's own average_order_value metric the lift reads +13.8%, since that metric excludes post-order adjustments.

Open — not yet answerable
Cohort LTV cannot judge the June 7 change for about two more weeks.

Through month 1 — the only maturity where all three relevant cohorts are complete — the June cohort is ahead: $59.34 per customer against $56.17 (May) and $57.65 (April). Initial order value per customer also rose across the same cohorts, $46.03 → $46.32 → $48.38.

But month 2 for the June cohort and month 1 for the July cohort both land in August, which is only ~52% elapsed. Neither is readable yet. The first clean month-2 read on a post-change cohort arrives at the start of September.

The volume side is not ambiguous. New customers acquired: April 12,527 → May 8,310 → June 9,000 → July 6,406. July is down 29% against June. Slightly better per-customer economics on materially fewer customers is not obviously a win, and whether it nets out depends on what happened to CAC — which is not in this dataset.
